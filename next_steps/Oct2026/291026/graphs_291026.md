# The eleven DAGs (29 October 2026)

The graphs of [part1_291026.md](part1_291026.md), its figures and the slides (F7). Every one has the form

$$
M^\ast=-\mathrm{diag}(d)+\varepsilon B,\qquad M_{ij}\ne0\ (i\ne j)\ \Leftrightarrow\ \text{edge }j\to i,
$$

with one weight $\varepsilon$ on every edge and $d_i=1+\mathrm{depth}(i)$, $\mathrm{depth}(i)$ the number of edges on the longest
directed path into $i$. So $d_t\lt d_h$ on every edge $t\to h$, from the tail $t$ (the parent) to the head $h$ (the child):
the parent decays more slowly. Only the close chain has other rates. How the data
are drawn: [data_generation_291026.md](data_generation_291026.md).

## Chains

```text
chain 1→2→3      1 → 2 → 3                d = (1, 2, 3)
chain of 4       1 → 2 → 3 → 4            d = (1, 2, 3, 4)
chain of 5       1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5        d = (1, 2, 3, 4, 5)
chain, close d   1 → 2 → 3                d = (1, 1.2, 1.44)
```

Each node drives the next one, and no other edges exist.

$$
M_{\mathrm{chain3}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\ \varepsilon&-2&0\\ 0&\varepsilon&-3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M_{\mathrm{close}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\ \varepsilon&-1.2&0\\ 0&\varepsilon&-1.44\end{pmatrix}
$$

The chains of 4 and 5 extend $M_{\mathrm{chain3}}$: diagonal $-1,\dots,-p$, and $\varepsilon$ just below it.

**Close chain.** The same graph as the chain 1→2→3, with rates close together: each is $1.2$ times the one before.
So $d_t/d_h=1/1.2\approx0.83$ on both edges, against $0.5$ and $0.67$ in the chain 1→2→3. Near the diagonal, with the
data not standardised, $\rho$, $\rho_{\mathrm{weak}}$ and $1-t^\ast$ all approach $\max_{t\to h}d_t/d_h$ (step 1 of 26 October,
tested), so the lasso's conditions fail at a smaller $\varepsilon$.

## Parallel paths

```text
3 parallel paths    1 → 2 → 5         diamond    1 → 2 → 4
                    1 → 3 → 5                    1 → 3 → 4
                    1 → 4 → 5
d = (1, 2, 2, 2, 3)                   d = (1, 2, 2, 3)
```

Node 1 reaches the last node along three (or two) directed paths, and the shortcut from 1 to the last node is not an
edge.

$$
M_{\mathrm{par3}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0&0&0\\ \varepsilon&-2&0&0&0\\ \varepsilon&0&-2&0&0\\ \varepsilon&0&0&-2&0\\ 0&\varepsilon&\varepsilon&\varepsilon&-3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M_{\mathrm{diamond}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0&0\\ \varepsilon&-2&0&0\\ \varepsilon&0&-2&0\\ 0&\varepsilon&\varepsilon&-3\end{pmatrix}
$$

**One negative edge** (`par3_neg`, `diamond_neg`): the same graphs with $M_{21}=-\varepsilon$, so the path through node 2 has
the opposite sign to the other paths.

- Why: with equal signs the paths reinforce each other. The fit the lasso loses by shrinking them moves onto the missing
  shortcut, and $\rho_{\mathrm{weak}}>1$ already from $\varepsilon=0.18$ (paths) and $0.28$ (diamond). With one path of the opposite sign
  this partly cancels: $\rho_{\mathrm{weak}}\lt1$ up to $1.08$ and $1.50$.
- Why $1\to2$: no reason. A minus on any one edge of one path gives the same $\rho$, $\rho_{\mathrm{weak}}$ and $t^\ast$ (checked
  for $2\to5$ and $1\to3$), since flipping the sign of a node's variable flips every edge at that node. What counts is
  the sign of each path, the product of its edge signs.

## Two parents, two children, a shortcut

```text
collider    1 → 3 ← 2          d = (1, 1, 2)
fork        2 ← 1 → 3          d = (1, 2, 2)
triangle    1 → 2 → 3, 1 → 3   d = (1, 2, 3)
```

- Collider: two parents without parents of their own (so $d_1=d_2=1$), one common child.
- Fork: one common parent of two children, which are not joined by an edge.
- Triangle: the chain 1→2→3 plus its shortcut 1→3, the complete DAG on three nodes. Its rate $d_3=3$ comes from the
  longer path 1→2→3.

$$
M_{\mathrm{collider}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\ 0&-1&0\\ \varepsilon&\varepsilon&-2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M_{\mathrm{fork}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\ \varepsilon&-2&0\\ \varepsilon&0&-2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M_{\mathrm{triangle}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\ \varepsilon&-2&0\\ \varepsilon&\varepsilon&-3\end{pmatrix}
$$
